What is the Kelly Criterion? (plain terms)

Every trade you take has two things attached to it: how often you win (win rate), and how much you make when you win vs. lose when you lose (your R multiple). The Kelly Criterion answers: what fraction of my capital should I risk per trade to grow my account as fast as possible, without eventually blowing it up?

Formula: f* = W/a − (1−W)/b, where W = win rate, b = average winning R multiple, a = average losing R magnitude (usually close to 1R, since that's what your stop-loss defines).

The curve above is your expected long-run growth rate as risk-per-trade increases. Its peak is f* — the mathematical point of maximum return. Risking more than that doesn't add extra long-run return, it only adds risk, and growth eventually turns negative. Because real-world win-rate and R estimates are noisy and this account will have relatively few trades logged, most practitioners size at a fraction of full Kelly — commonly half-Kelly — trading a little theoretical growth for much less volatility and drawdown.
